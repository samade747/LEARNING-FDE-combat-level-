### Q1

A clinic manager opens an assistant and types "build me a system for following up with patients
who miss appointments." The output looks impressive but nobody can use it. Which competency
failed, and why?

A. Delegation, because nobody had decided who may be contacted, after how long, in what tone, and
which decisions a human must keep
B. Discernment, because she accepted a polished answer without checking any of its factual claims
against a reliable source
C. Description, because the request was too short, and a longer, better-worded prompt would have
produced a usable answer on the first try
D. Diligence, because patient follow-up is regulated work and a consumer AI service was never
approved for use with clinical records

**Answer: A.** The failure happened before the first prompt. Which patients, how late, what tone,
and what needs human approval are clinic policy questions — no amount of prompting decides them.
_Source: 3. Delegation._

### Q2

The framework defines agency this way: a human configures AI to independently perform future
tasks, including for others, on their behalf. Which detail separates agency from automation most
sharply?

A. Agency needs a more capable model, while automation runs well enough on smaller, cheaper models
B. Agency means the AI picks its own goals instead of goals a person set
C. Agency always connects AI to outside tools and live data, automation only works on pasted text
D. You are not in the room while the work happens, and the person served may not be you, so
judgment is built in beforehand

**Answer: D.** The words _future_ and _for others_ carry the weight. Model size and "AI picks its
own goals" are not part of the definition. _Source: 2. Three ways to work with AI._

### Q3

A team ships an AI tutor. Every individual answer it gives is factually correct, yet students
perform worse than before. Which kind of discernment would have caught this?

A. Product discernment — checks each output for factual errors, missing requirements,
contradictions
B. Process discernment — asks whether working alongside the AI is still paying off for the team
C. Performance discernment — asks whether AI's independent behavior serves the people meeting it,
shown across many cases
D. Creation discernment — checks training data and connected systems suited the audience

**Answer: C.** Correct answers with worse outcomes point to a behavior problem, not an accuracy
problem — it shows up in what users do next, not any single output. _Source: 5.3 Performance
discernment._

### Q4

A writer has spent forty minutes steering an assistant through a 600-word article she could have
drafted in thirty. The final piece is good. What should she conclude?

A. The process is failing even though the product is fine, and her moves are a new performance
description, another tool, or taking it back
B. The session succeeded, because a good final product is what matters
C. She has a description problem — write a much fuller brief before drafting
D. She has a delegation problem — split the article into smaller AI-assigned pieces

**Answer: A.** This is process discernment — steering that costs more than it saves is a loss
counted as a win because it felt productive. _Source: 5.2 Process discernment._

### Q5

You tell an assistant: "do not agree with me just to be polite, and flag anything you are
uncertain about." This small habit is a scaled-down version of what?

A. Product description — specifying qualities the final output must have
B. Process description — prescribing the order of steps the AI should follow
C. Context engineering — adding standing info the AI keeps using on later turns
D. Performance description — defining behavior you have not seen yet, which at scale becomes an
agent's standing rules

**Answer: D.** You are writing a rule for behavior that has not happened yet, for an audience of
one — move the stakes and the same sentence governs a thousand strangers. _Source: 4.3 Performance
description._

### Q6

An AI recommends Vendor A, and the recommendation is sound. On inspection, the AI believed Vendor A
ships a feature that does not exist. What does the course say about this?

A. The recommendation stands — discernment judges usefulness, and the output was the right call
B. This is a hallucination and therefore a Diligence failure
C. The AI's stated reasoning is a true record of its own internal computation
D. A right answer resting on a wrong assumption will not stay right, so judging the result includes
judging the case made for it

**Answer: D.** A right answer for wrong reasons will break on the next decision that depends on
the same assumption. What you get back is a justification offered for review, not a record of
hidden reasoning. _Source: 5.1 Product discernment._

### Q7

A lecturer pastes student names, grades, and disciplinary notes into a consumer AI service the
university never approved. The feedback it drafts is excellent. What does the course conclude?

A. The use is irresponsible whatever the output quality — the failure is creation diligence: the
wrong data went into an unapproved tool
B. The use is acceptable because he reviewed every comment before sending
C. The failure is transparency diligence — disclosing afterwards would resolve it
D. The failure is discernment — he should have judged accuracy before allowing it into a record

**Answer: A.** Creation diligence governs what you put in and where. Good output does not repair a
bad choice of tool and data. _Source: 6.1 Creation diligence._

### Q8

The course says AI can automate work but cannot automate accountability. Which situation
illustrates that most directly?

A. A model produces different answers on different days, so a team cannot reproduce a result
B. A coding assistant introduces a security flaw, an engineer ships it, and the engineer and
organization still own the consequences
C. An assistant refuses a request outside its safety guidelines
D. An agent escalates far more cases than expected, erasing the time savings

**Answer: B.** The AI wrote the flaw and the humans still carry it — responsibility does not
transfer along with the task. _Source: 6.3 Deployment diligence._

### Q9

Why does the course open by saying a new colleague and an AI assistant are alike in one way and
different in another?

A. AI is faster, so there's no need to plan ahead
B. A colleague can refuse an unreasonable request; AI will attempt anything
C. Both need context you forgot to give, but AI keeps nothing between chats — what a person hears
once, you must supply every time
D. A colleague improves over months, AI improves only within a session and then stops for good

**Answer: C.** The shared problem is missing context. The difference is memory: a colleague still
knows next month, AI opens every chat with someone who has never met you. _Source: Opening, and 4.
Description._

### Q10

Ayesha's team tests a reconciliation agent against past reconciliations the firm already trusts.
An accountant also reviews some matches that appear to have succeeded, not only the failures. Why
review the successes?

A. To measure how much faster the agent is than the manual process
B. Because a system can look safe when it fails without saying so, and a silent, repeated error
hides among the successes that look fine
C. Because reviewing successes satisfies transparency diligence
D. Because accuracy on correct cases decides the escalation threshold

**Answer: B.** A silent failure looks exactly like a success from the outside — so the sample has
to include cases that appear fine. _Source: 7. The 4Ds as a practical operating loop._

### Q11

A reader compares two requests. The first is "Summarize this report." The second names the
audience, the time they have, the topics to cover, the format, and what to leave out. What is the
lesson?

A. The longer request works because length itself signals seriousness
B. The second one is not more intelligent, it is more complete, and completeness usually matters
far more than clever wording does
C. The second one works because it uses the vocabulary of the field
D. The pair shows summarizing is a poor use of AI

**Answer: B.** Completeness, not cleverness or length, is the point. _Source: 4.1 Product
description._

### Q12

Where does the course place Diligence in the 10-80-10 Rule, and why does the placement matter?

A. In the final 10%, because verifying is the last gate before anything ships
B. In the first 10%, because data/tool/approval decisions must be settled before AI is involved
C. Across all 100%, because it surrounds the whole workflow rather than sitting at any one stage,
and is never a final checkbox
D. In the middle 80%, because it is exercised continuously while steering AI

**Answer: C.** Delegation and Description are strongest at the front, Discernment becomes critical
at the end, Diligence runs across the whole thing — treating it as a final gate is the fourth
beginner mistake the course names. _Source: How this connects to the Four Survival Skills and the
10-80-10 Rule._
