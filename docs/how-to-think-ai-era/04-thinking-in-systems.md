# 04 — Discipline 4: Thinking in Systems

University ne in-person tutoring ka kuch hissa AI chatbot se replace karne ka decision kiya —
AI ne bola "30% save hoga." 6 mahine baad struggling students aana band ho gaye (chatbot unke
half-formed questions samajh nahi pata), grades gir gaye, parents complain karne lage, university
ne **zyada** tutors hire kiye — original budget se zyada cost hui. "30% save" paper par correct
tha. **Chain reaction ne saving wipe kar di.**

**Failure mode:** AI pucha hua sawal answer kar ke ruk jata — "kitna save hoga?" — aur side effects
almost kabhi trace nahi karta. Effect A → B → C, aur kabhi C circle back kar ke decision undo kar
deti.

**Cascade Map** = wo drawing jo tum khud banate ho yeh chains trace karne ke liye, commit karne se
**pehle** — surprise paper par ho, 6-mahine-baad budget mein nahi.

**"Thinking in systems" kyun?** System = parts ka set jo ek-doosre ko affect karte. Zyadatar log
straight lines mein reason karte: A causes B, khatam. Par system ke parts circles mein connected
hain — effect ghoom ke wapas aakar us cheez ko change kar sakta jisne shuru kiya.

## Map Banane Ke 5 Steps (~20 Min Pehli Baar, ~10 Min Baad Mein)

1. **Decision ek clear sentence mein likho.** Specific — "shayad tutoring change karo" nahi, "half
   in-person tutoring hours AI chatbot se replace karo next semester" .
2. **5 groups list karo jo decision affect karti.** Workers (tutors), users (students), competitors
   (doosri universities), rules (policies), aur team ka knowledge-gap (chatbot actually kitna
   achha hai?).
3. **Har group ke liye "aur phir kya?" 3 baar pucho.** First thing, phir uska effect, phir uska
   effect. 3 layers deep.
4. **Jahan effects wapas aate hain dhoondo.** **Loop** = chain jahan later effect wapas circle
   karke original decision ko worse (kabhi better) banati. Kam az kam 1 dhoondo, specific **kaise**
   likho.
5. **Map clean/simple lage to bohot jaldi roke.** Real risks 2nd/3rd layers mein hote. Tab tak push
   karo jab tak messy na lage.

## Worked Example — Struggling Students

- **1st layer:** struggling students chatbot try karte, samajh nahi pata, help maangna band
- **2nd layer:** grades girte, jinhe sabse zyada support chahiye tha unhe sabse kam mila
- **3rd layer:** kuch doosri university transfer karte jahan human tutors hain, tuition loss

**Loop:** lost tuition → tighter budget → tutoring ke liye kam paisa → chatbot aur zyada cover
karta → aur zyada students give up. **Original decision apne aap ko feed karti.**

## AI + Tum — Opposite Blind Spots

AI pucha hua sawal achhe se answer karta, side effects notice karne mein bad hai. Tum logon aur
chain-reactions jo months mein khulti unko better soch sakte ho. Isliye: **tum pehle map draw
karo, phir AI se har branch stress-test karwao.**

## Worked Example — Loan Officers Replace Karna

5 domains: Employees (job loss — obvious), Customers (worse service — obvious), Competitors
(pressure to follow — obvious), Regulators (fairness scrutiny), **Internal knowledge (tacit local
lore lost — easy to miss!)**. Tacit local lore = staff ka unwritten knowledge (kaunsa local
business thin-credit ke sath bhi reliable hai, kiska income seasonal hai) — officers chale jaate,
yeh knowledge software mein kabhi nahi thi.

**Loop (Customers domain se):** cost cut → service drops (AI cues miss karta) → customers leave →
revenue drops → savings vanish. **Cost-cutting move apni hi justification erase kar deti.**

## Another Example — Sports Day (Appendix)

Student council president stadium se university-ground shift karna chahti thi, 40% budget save
karne ke liye. 3rd layer: ground kam spectators hold karta → fewer families → event chhota lagta →
sponsors kam paise dete next year → budget shrink → event aur chhota. **Loop: shrinking event every
year, bina kisi ne decide kiye.** Usne 2 protections add kiye: guaranteed minimum sponsor package +
spectator-capacity check commit karne se pehle.

## Research Ke Peeche

- **Bullwhip effect (Jay Forrester, 1958):** chain mein ek point ka decision outward ripple karta,
  distorted wapas aata — retail mein chhota demand change factory orders mein bare swings banata.
- **Misperception of feedback (John Sterman, Beer Game):** MBA students/executives bhi reliably
  bare swings banate — delays/loops jo dikh nahi rahe react karte waqt. Loops invisible hain jab
  tak force na karo draw karne ke liye.
- **Leverage points (Donella Meadows, 2008):** system change karne ki sabse powerful jagah almost
  kabhi obvious nahi hoti — usually feedback loop hoti, jo straight-line analysis kabhi naam nahi
  leti. Jo loop draw nahi ki, usay adjust/protect nahi kar sakte.
